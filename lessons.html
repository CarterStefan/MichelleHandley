Your first lesson

What to expect

No auditions and no judgement. Here’s how a first lesson usually goes.

  1. A chat

    We’ll talk about what you love to sing, what you’d like to achieve and any experience you already have.

  2. A gentle warm-up

    Simple breathing and vocal exercises, so I can hear your voice and you can feel how it works.

  3. A song you love

    We’ll sing something you’ve chosen, and I’ll show you one or two small changes that make a real difference.

  4. A plan

    You’ll leave with a few exercises to practise and a clear idea of where lessons could take you.

Questions

Lesson FAQs

Do I need to be able to read music?

Not at all. Most of my students don’t when they start. If you’d like to learn, we can build it in gradually, but it’s never a requirement.

What age can students start?

I teach students from age [PLACEHOLDER: age]. Young voices are still developing, so lessons for children focus on healthy habits, confidence and having fun.

What should I bring?

A bottle of water, any songs you’d like to work on, and something to jot notes on. For online lessons, headphones help too.

How often should I have lessons?

Weekly lessons give the steadiest progress, but fortnightly works well for busy adults. We’ll find a rhythm that suits you.

Can you prepare me for a singing exam?

Yes. I prepare students for [PLACEHOLDER: exam boards] graded exams and can help you choose the syllabus that suits your voice and goals.

Are you checked to work with children?

Yes. I hold an up-to-date enhanced background check. [PLACEHOLDER: confirm safeguarding check] Parents and guardians are always welcome to sit in on lessons for under-18s.

I’m really nervous. Is that normal?

Completely. Almost everyone is nervous at their first lesson, and it passes quickly. You’re in a private room with someone whose whole job is to help you, not to judge you.
